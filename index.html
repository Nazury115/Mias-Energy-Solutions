The starting point

After a recommendation, where do you look?

My father’s business relies on word of mouth. When someone hears the name, there is no company website where they can see the range of work in one place.

What I wanted a visitor to find

“Does Mias handle the kind of work I need?”

“Can I see an example?”

“What information should I send to get started?”

Pacing: 1 min · Cumulative: 2:00

  • Explain why word of mouth matters to a family business. A recommendation gives a person a name they can trust.
  • Use a hypothetical visitor who hears about Mias for a water pump. That person might also need electrical work and have no reason to know Mias offers it.
  • Explain the goal in my own words. Easier discovery could lead to more inquiries and paying customers. Measuring that comes later.

Project vision

What I asked GPT and Claude to design

My contribution

I defined what visitors should be able to do and what the site should show about my father’s business.

I wanted services, completed projects and product information to be easy to find.

How I used AI

GPT and Claude designed the site from my descriptions. The local preview gives me something concrete to show and evaluate.

For example, the water services page describes borehole pumps and lets a visitor request an assessment.

Pacing: 1 min · Cumulative: 3:00

  • Describe the brief I gave GPT and Claude. Start with the visitor’s questions from the previous slide.
  • Explain why services and projects need different treatment. A service describes what Mias offers. A project story needs verified details about work that actually happened. Sample stories in the preview carry a fictional label.
  • Use borehole pumps as the example of equipment information within a service page.

How the app works

What happens when someone sends an inquiry?

Browser

Describe the job

The contact form collects the request and the visitor’s preferred reply method.

Next.js · React · TypeScript

Server

Check and save

The server checks the details and writes the inquiry to the local database.

Server actions · PGlite

Staff workspace

Follow up

Staff see the saved inquiry and can turn it into a job with a responsible person and a next step.

The visitor gets a reference once the save succeeds. If it fails, the form displays the problem.

Pacing: 1 min 30 sec · Cumulative: 4:30

  • Start with the browser. Think of the contact form as the place where a customer describes the work in their own words.
  • Trace the submission into the database. A reference tells the visitor that staff can look up the request. Email and SMS notifications are currently captured on the local machine.
  • Explain the private side. A customer needs an invitation to view a job. Staff can share updates while keeping internal notes private.

App walkthrough

Finding help with a borehole pump

Example visitor journey

  1. Open Water solutions and find the borehole pump service.
  2. Choose Discuss water solutions and describe the site and the help needed.
  3. Submit the inquiry and keep the reference for follow-up.

Pacing: 1 min 30 sec · Cumulative: 6:00

  • Walk through this hypothetical example with the screenshot. The person knows they need help with a pump but may not know the specifications.
  • Point out the service description and the contact button. Explain why asking about the site is a useful next step for that visitor.
  • Once staff accept the request, they can invite a customer to see job progress and messages. Staff can also prepare a quotation for Owner review.

Preliminary results

What I can demonstrate today

Public website

Visitors can browse three service families, explore the project gallery and submit an inquiry.

Job portal

Staff can accept a request, update its progress and share files with an invited customer.

Document review

Staff can prepare quotations or invoices. An Owner can approve a version for download.

For the document feature, the project recorded 180 passing unit tests and two end-to-end Edge browser journeys at that milestone.

Pacing: 1 min 30 sec · Cumulative: 7:30

  • Describe the preliminary app as ready to demonstrate locally. Point to a specific action each user can perform.
  • Explain a meaningful browser test. The invited customer could open the issued document, while an unrelated account was denied access. This checks something that matters to a real business.
  • The counts come from the increment 010 verification record. Documents use test references. I have not collected user feedback yet, so the next question is how people experience the app.

Impact and next steps

How I would tell whether the site helps

First, watch someone use it

Ask a classmate to find a pump service and start an inquiry. Watch where they pause and ask what information they expected.

Ask my father to check the services, equipment descriptions and project details against the business.

After a public launch

Track how people discover the site, how many make inquiries and which inquiries become paid jobs.

Revenue would show what those jobs earned. Profit would also depend on the costs of delivering them and running the site.

Pacing: 1 min 30 sec · Cumulative: 9:00

  • I have not received feedback yet. The classroom demonstration is a chance to get a first reaction.
  • Give classmates a task without explaining the navigation first. Their first click and any questions will show what to improve.
  • Explain my longer-term hope. More visibility could bring more customers, revenue and profit. I would need actual visits, completed jobs and costs before I could say that happened.

Questions and feedback
