16-week roadmap

Eight phases with approval gates

Check off a week when its deliverables are verified, documented and understood. Progress is stored only in this browser.

1

Discovery, requirements & foundation

Understand the business problem before deciding what to build.

Weeks 1–2
Week 01

Business discovery

Collect evidence, define users and agree on the business problem.

  • Owner/stakeholder interview
  • Project brief and business goals
  • Target users, needs and pain points
  • Content and asset inventory
  • Assumption, unknown and risk register
  • Week 1 engineering journal
Week 02

Scope and foundation

Translate discovery into an approved, testable project definition.

  • Functional and non-functional requirements
  • User journeys and conversion paths
  • MVP definition and sitemap
  • Competitor analysis: structure, trust, mobile, SEO and conversion
  • Mobile and desktop wireframes
  • Technology evaluation, repository and basic deployment pipeline
✓
Discovery approval gate

Proceed only when we can answer: who are we building for, what problem are we solving, and what is the smallest credible website that solves it?

2

Design system & architecture

Create clear visual rules and a maintainable technical structure.

Weeks 3–4
Week 03

Visual foundation

Define an engineering-company identity that remains clear and usable on small screens.

  • Confirmed palette and typography
  • Spacing, grid and responsive rules
  • Buttons, forms, navigation and footer
  • Service, project and CTA card patterns
  • Focus, error, hover and reduced-motion states
  • Accessibility rules documented
Week 04

Technical architecture

Approve only the technology needed for the defined product.

  • Folder and component strategy
  • Structured content model
  • Image and media strategy
  • SEO and metadata architecture
  • Framework and content-management ADRs
  • Reusable components verified across breakpoints
✓
Architecture approval gate

Human approval is required for framework, content management, data storage, external services, recurring cost and deployment architecture.

3

Core website

Build the smallest complete customer-facing experience.

Weeks 5–7
Week 05

Homepage

Let visitors identify the business, services and next action within seconds.

  • Hero and value proposition
  • Service overview
  • Evidence-based reasons to consider Mias
  • Featured-project structure
  • Primary and final conversion CTAs
  • Mobile navigation and responsive verification
Week 06

Services

Explain each confirmed service in practical customer language.

  • Services overview page
  • Reusable service-page template
  • Problem, audience and solution guidance
  • Service-specific contact paths
  • Internal links and descriptive URLs
  • Semantic structure and image placeholders
Week 07

About, projects & contact

Complete the credibility and inquiry journey without unsupported claims.

  • About / Why Mias page
  • Projects/gallery structure
  • Clearly labelled development placeholders
  • Contact page and contact options
  • Basic form interface
  • Confirmed public business details only
✓
Core experience gate

A user can navigate every primary page on mobile and begin an inquiry. No invented testimonials, projects, credentials or statistics appear.

4

Content, SEO & local search

Turn verified business information into useful, discoverable content.

Weeks 8–9
Week 08

Content development

Write concise, credible copy based on customer needs and confirmed facts.

  • Homepage and company positioning copy
  • Service-page content
  • Contact and CTA microcopy
  • Project-content template
  • Owner fact-check and approval
  • Content inventory updated
Week 09

Technical and local SEO

Make pages understandable to search engines without keyword stuffing.

  • Titles, descriptions and canonical strategy
  • Open Graph metadata
  • Sitemap and robots configuration
  • LocalBusiness schema using confirmed facts
  • Alt text and internal-link review
  • SEO strategy documentation
✓
Content approval gate

The business owner approves all published claims, contact information and project material before it can be considered production-ready.

5

Lead generation & interactivity

Build secure, understandable inquiry flows with useful feedback.

Weeks 10–11
Week 10

Contact-form architecture

Select and implement the simplest reliable submission path.

  • Contact-form ADR
  • Minimal field and privacy review
  • Client- and server-side validation
  • Secure environment variables
  • Loading, success and error states
  • Submission delivery verified end to end
Week 11

Conversion details

Reduce friction while avoiding unnecessary interactive features.

  • WhatsApp links with appropriate context
  • Click-to-call behavior
  • Service-specific quote CTAs
  • Spam and rate-limit protection
  • Analytics event preparation
  • Evidence-based decision on service selector
✓
Security and service approval gate

Approve form provider/backend, stored data, privacy behavior, anti-spam measures, analytics and any recurring cost before production use.

6

Testing, accessibility & performance

Measure the experience, fix real problems and document evidence.

Weeks 12–13
Week 12

Systematic QA

Test critical journeys on representative devices, viewports and browsers.

  • Mobile, desktop and browser matrix
  • Navigation, link and form tests
  • Keyboard and focus testing
  • Error-state and empty-state testing
  • Metadata and structured-data checks
  • Defect log with severity and resolution
Week 13

Evidence-led optimization

Use measured bottlenecks to improve performance and accessibility.

  • Lighthouse baseline and final results
  • Image, font and JavaScript review
  • Layout-shift and loading analysis
  • Contrast and screen-reader checks
  • WCAG 2.2 AA practice review
  • Testing and accessibility documentation
✓
Quality gate

Critical journeys work, severe issues are resolved, and performance/accessibility claims are supported by recorded test results.

7

Production deployment & launch

Connect real business information and prove that inquiries arrive.

Weeks 14–15
Week 14

Production preparation

Configure a secure production environment and remove development placeholders.

  • Domain and HTTPS configuration
  • Production environment variables
  • Real contact and service information
  • Approved project assets
  • Analytics and search readiness
  • Launch and recovery checklists
Week 15

Soft launch and public launch

Test with real stakeholders, verify lead delivery and release deliberately.

  • Soft-launch stakeholder review
  • Final content and device QA
  • Real WhatsApp, phone and form tests
  • Search-engine readiness check
  • Owner launch approval
  • Production launch and monitoring
✓
Launch approval gate

No public launch until the owner approves content and a real test inquiry successfully reaches the responsible person.

Week 16

✓
Final definition of done

The product satisfies its requirements, works across relevant devices, has tested error behavior, includes documented decisions and can be confidently explained by the human developer.

Working agreement

Human in the loop

AI may accelerate research and implementation, but important product and engineering decisions remain visible, reviewed and owned by the developer.

  1. UnderstandRestate the objective and expected outcome.
  2. Define acceptance criteriaAgree what must be true for completion.
  3. Identify decisionsSurface product, UX, security, accessibility and performance choices.
  4. Consider alternativesCompare realistic options, cost, complexity and maintenance.
  5. Recommend and approveExplain the fit; pause for major human decisions.
  6. Implement incrementallyBuild the smallest complete change.
  7. VerifyRun proportional tests; do not infer success from generated code.
  8. Document and teachRecord the decision, explain the implementation and check understanding.
MVP boundary

Launch essentials before enhancements

A focused first release protects the schedule and gives the business a useful product sooner.

Included in production MVP

  • Home, services, About / Why Mias, projects/gallery and contact
  • Reusable service pages and responsive navigation
  • WhatsApp, click-to-call and validated quote/contact form
  • Accessible semantic HTML and optimized images
  • SEO, local SEO, metadata, structured data, sitemap and robots
  • Secure submission handling and basic spam protection
  • Production deployment, HTTPS and documentation

Deferred until justified

  • Quote estimator or multi-step wizard
  • Automated service recommendation
  • Customer accounts or payments
  • Advanced CMS or database
  • Blog and resource library
  • Analytics dashboards
  • Complex animation and multilingual support
First human task · 45–60 minutes

Interview the business owner

Capture exact answers about business identity, priority services, customers, inquiry handling, evidence, content, legal constraints and launch ownership.

  • Record facts exactly as given.
  • Do not convert opinions into claims.
  • Mark every uncertain answer TODO: Confirm with business owner.
  • Return the answers for review before application development begins.

Business

A real customer can discover Mias, understand its services, trust the evidence presented and initiate an inquiry quickly.

Engineering

Ownership

The developer can explain the architecture, decisions, data flow, trade-offs, verification, AI collaboration and lessons learned.
